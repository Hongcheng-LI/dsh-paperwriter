## Methods that explain the work without reproducing the notebook

Design Methods around the study's actual operations and the information needed to reproduce or interpret them. Distinguish shared instruments and conventions from procedure-specific settings. Put repeated compositions or parameter sets in a readable table when useful; place extended protocols and computational records in SI when appropriate, retaining explicit access and cross-references. Do not delete essential reproducibility information merely because it is not the paper's novelty.

Choose detail by consequence, not by how much source text is available. For example, a comparison may need sample preparation and normalization more than routine handling narration; an isolation needs a traceable material-to-fraction-to-product route; a calculation needs the actual stages and parameter meanings rather than just a software name and final value. Preserve unusual verified procedures rather than silently replacing them with conventional ones. Adapt these principles to the discipline instead of requiring every example in every Methods section.

Draft concise connected method paragraphs from confirmed information. Avoid copying notebook shorthand or internal phrases such as “reported concentration” into clean prose. For a genuinely missing essential parameter, use a short identifiable placeholder linked to a review note; keep long requests and conditional future protocols outside the manuscript. Do not add optional measurements or empty characterization fields simply to make a template look complete. Do not announce an SI table or dataset as present until it exists; while planning, label the destination as proposed.

Guide completion through targeted retrieval. First look in supplied records for the answer; if it remains missing, tell the author which specific record would resolve it and why it matters to the passage. Prioritize blockers to interpretation or reproducibility over optional refinements. When new evidence arrives, update the affected wording and related sections, retire resolved questions, and continue drafting rather than repeat the full intake checklist.

## Reason from the described procedure before editing it

Do not stop at listing missing parameters. Read the paragraph literally as an executable sequence: what material enters each step, what changes, which preparation reaches the measurement, and how that measurement becomes the reported result. Look for places where the prose may conflate distinct operations, such as a stock versus a final solution, a counting dilution versus an assay inoculum, or parallel wells versus independent experiments. Treat these as alternative interpretations to resolve, not errors already proved.

Use the provided quantities to expose consequential implications. When relevant, calculate volume fractions, dilution factors, final concentrations or material balances with an available calculator or code, retaining units and assumptions. For example, adding 10 μL of a solution to a final volume of 200 μL gives a 5% volume contribution; it implies 5% solvent only if the added solution is entirely that solvent and other sources are accounted for. Check whether the implication changes across treatment groups. Do not infer an unknown starting concentration, assume a conventional protocol was used, or overwrite an unusual recorded value.

Prioritize the ambiguity that most changes interpretation or the next writing decision. Explain the chain concisely: “This wording implies X; if instead Y happened, the method and its interpretation differ; record Z would distinguish them.” First inspect available record Z. If still unresolved, ask the author a specific question about the operation, not merely “please verify the method.” Where useful, state how each possible answer changes the prose. Do not require the author to answer every secondary formatting question before improving the supported parts.

Connect the measurement to the endpoint: identify the actual rule that turns an instrument reading or observation into the reported outcome, including the relevant comparator and treatment of repetitions. A familiar endpoint name is not a substitute for that rule. Verify specialized methodological claims against appropriate original guidance when needed; distinguish concerns derived from the supplied text from externally established requirements.

Then provide editorial help proportionate to the request: a recommendation for an assessment question, or a revised paragraph when revision is requested. Keep conditional alternatives outside clean manuscript text. This reasoning should lead to a better passage and a useful next author action, not become a standalone audit that displaces the writing task.
