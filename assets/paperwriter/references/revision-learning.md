## Learn from revisions of this skill's own drafts

When an author provides an earlier draft and another editor's revision, establish their provenance. If the earlier draft was generated by this skill from the author's data, treat its writing defects as first-draft performance feedback, not as shortcomings of the author's writing. Compare what the revision retained, removed, relocated, reordered or clarified, and identify which drafting decision should have produced that improvement originally.

Separate writing gains from newly supplied evidence and unsupported additions. A smoother revision is not automatically a more accurate source. Reuse the successful editorial principle, not case-specific facts or every recommendation in the response. If a screenshot, spectrum or calculation file is mentioned but absent, do not claim to have inspected it or propagate its purported correction as verified project data.

For a behavior check, use a bounded passage with the same available evidence and assess whether the revised approach produces better focus, clearer organization, proportionate detail and a useful next author question without new factual claims. Present it as a demonstration unless an actual independent comparison was performed. Do not claim writing improvement from a syntax validator or from adding guidance alone.
